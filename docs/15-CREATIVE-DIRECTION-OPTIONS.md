# 15 — DAG Creative Direction Options

**Research and proposal date:** 6 November 2026  
**Status:** Two visual territories proposed; no owner approval recorded.  
**Recommended direction:** Territory A, with carefully rationed colour and merchandising energy from Territory B.

## The brief in one sentence

Build a contemporary, product-led gallery that lets the material character of verified Jaisalmer/Rajasthan pieces carry the emotion, while giving a Japan-first customer the detail and confidence needed to understand and purchase them.

This is not a Japanese-themed storefront. Japanese principles guide service, rhythm and restraint; DAG's visual identity must come from its own place, collection and photography.

## What the reference review actually found

### POJ Studio — active global craft retail reference

Reviewed on 6 November 2026:
- The homepage presents a clear mission and campaign entry point, then links product, artisan and editorial narratives rather than treating the journal as a separate unrelated content site.
- Navigation separates Shop, Experiences, Kintsugi, Stories and About; the shop itself organizes a large catalogue by categories and bestsellers.
- The inspected Shop All page showed 296 products, category links, filters for availability and price, sort controls, visible variant information, and explicit states such as Sold out and Preorder.
- The site exposes country/currency and language controls; the inspected configuration exposed Japan/JPY and English/Japanese options. A top notice also links tariff/duty information.
- The Kintsugi collection and journal demonstrate a focused collection landing page alongside practical educational stories.

DAG can learn from the separation of product taxonomy, artisan context, education and service information; from visible availability/variant states; and from making localization and duties discoverable. DAG must not copy POJ's category taxonomy, copy, product imagery, page layouts or brand language. POJ has an active 296-product catalogue; DAG's catalogue structure is still to be verified from the actual inventory.

### Macaroni Shoten — restrained everyday-curation reference, with a limitation

Reviewed on 6 November 2026:

## Territory A — Material Archive

**Position:** A contemporary gallery/archive where material, construction and individual pieces feel worthy of attention.  
**Role:** Recommended lead territory.

### Visual character

- Warm mineral-paper background, ink-dark typography and a small oxidized accent.
- Large, deliberate product photography with room for texture and close detail.
- Editorial serif display paired with a highly legible neutral sans-serif for navigation, specifications and transactional UI. Final typefaces remain unselected.
- Asymmetric editorial composition, but a disciplined and familiar catalogue grid once shopping begins.
- Calm surfaces with enough information density to avoid empty minimalism.

### Proposed exploration palette (not approved tokens)

| Token | Hex | Intended role |
|---|---|---|
| Mineral paper | `#F2EEE6` | Primary page surface |
| Pale limestone | `#D8CDBD` | Secondary surface and image surround |
| Ink | `#262521` | Main text and strong contrast |
| Oxide | `#A34F3C` | Small actions, markers and emphasis |
| Deep indigo | `#314653` | Secondary accent where supported by actual photography |
| Chalk | `#FBFAF7` | Card/surface contrast |

### Layout and merchandising

- Homepage opens with a specific, image-led collection proposition and a clear way into the catalogue. The image area must not become a blank luxury billboard.
- Category/collection modules use image, clear label and a concise editorial sentence only when it helps classification.
- Product listing uses a stable, responsive grid with visible product name, price when verified, and useful availability/variant state. Avoid hover-only information.
- Product detail gives the image gallery and factual product information equal importance: dimensions, material, condition, care, variations and provenance when known.
- Maker/story content supports a related product or collection path; it is not a mandatory long-form detour.

### Risks

- Can become too beige, too quiet or too editorial if product breadth and shopping controls are minimized.
- A refined serif can reduce legibility if applied to specifications or small UI.
- Neutral backdrops may make coloured textiles feel muted unless photography colour is carefully controlled.

## Territory B — Collected Colour

**Position:** A confident contemporary shop where the collection's colours and patterns provide the energy.  
**Role:** A more commercial, category-forward alternative.

### Visual character

- Warm white base and dark graphite text.
- Larger, more frequent accent blocks and a stronger visual cadence between collections.
- Product grids appear earlier; category and collection discovery receive more prominence.
- Neutral sans-serif UI with a restrained display face; typography does not compete with product pattern.
- Saturation comes primarily from authenticated product photography. Accent colours should not impersonate the products.

### Proposed exploration palette (not approved tokens)

| Token | Hex | Intended role |
|---|---|---|
| Warm white | `#F8F5EE` | Primary page surface |
| Graphite | `#242321` | Main text and UI |
| Madder | `#A94332` | Collection accent |
| Cobalt-indigo | `#304F70` | Secondary accent |
| Marigold | `#D3A044` | Limited highlight |
| Pale sand | `#E5D8C4` | Secondary surface |

### Layout and merchandising

- More compact homepage with visible category doors and a featured collection/product edit.
- Consistent product-card rhythm with stronger filtering and comparison cues.
- Collection intros remain short; products occupy a larger share of the first few screens.
- Use editorial modules between catalogue sections to explain material or context.

### Risks

- Can slip into generic marketplace density or a tourist-market aesthetic.
- Too many accent colours can overwhelm the actual textile/product palette.
- Strong grid repetition can make unique objects feel interchangeable.

## Recommendation — Material Archive with a Collected Colour merchandising layer

Use Territory A as the brand and page-composition foundation, but take three practical behaviours from Territory B:
1. Bring product/category discovery into the first viewport and keep it easy to reach.
2. Give the listing page enough useful information density for comparison; do not hide details behind hover or long editorial copy.
3. Use accent colour and collection rhythm to keep the store alive, not sparse.

This is the best balance between distinctiveness, product-led conversion and the owner's principle: **DO NOT ABANDON MINIMALISM. ABANDON EMPTINESS.**

## Photography direction

Do not decide photography treatment from stock images or broad assumptions about Rajasthan. Use the owner's 10–20 representative original images for the next visual test. The sample should include the real range of products, image quality, crop/orientation, scale and condition.

For that test:
- Preserve true colour and texture; no heavy tint or sepia overlay.
- Include a useful overall view plus close-up/detail and scale/context only when real images are available.
- Allow mixed portrait/landscape ratios where the source material requires them, with a consistent frame system rather than forcing every product into one crop.
- Use clear, literal alt text based on what is visible.
- Do not imply a maker, technique, origin, age or provenance not supported by product records.
