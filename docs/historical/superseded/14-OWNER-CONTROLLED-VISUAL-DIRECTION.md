# 14 — Owner-Controlled Visual Direction and Design Gate

## Purpose
Prevent AI agents from repeatedly choosing familiar neutral palettes and generic minimalist layouts. DAG's visual identity belongs to the owner. The agent researches, explores and presents options; the owner chooses.

## Non-negotiable rule
**The AI proposes. The owner chooses. The AI implements.**

The AI must not independently finalize brand palette, typography/font pairing, overall visual personality, art direction/photography mood, foundational layout language, background/surface treatment or product-card visual language where these materially define the brand.

## No automatic neutral default
Do not automatically choose grey, greige, beige, ivory, sand, muted terracotta, low-contrast monochrome or any palette inherited from the previous repository. Do not assume "Japanese-inspired" means neutral, pale, beige, minimal or muted.

These colours are not permanently prohibited. They require explicit owner selection like any other direction. Do not use a generic grey background for exploratory boards or plain wireframes as the only design deliverable.

## Required visual-choice package
Before production design-system implementation, create at least four materially different creative territories. The alternatives must differ meaningfully, not be four minor variations of the same palette.

For each territory show:
1. Name and one-sentence concept.
2. Exact palette with HEX values and roles: main background, secondary background, primary text, secondary text, two accents, surface/border.
3. Typography: display, heading, body and UI font suggestions; family, weights, hierarchy, availability and licensing.
4. Composition: page width/grid, header/navigation, hero, product card, category/collection and editorial section.
5. Art direction: photography mood, lighting, crop, texture, backgrounds and how actual textiles/objects are presented.
6. At least one rendered desktop and one rendered mobile example.
7. Advantages, disadvantages, likely customer impression and risks.
8. Accessibility/contrast implications.

## Make options genuinely different
Vary visual character and hue families. Possible exploration categories include a confident high-contrast palette with a saturated signature colour, a deep atmospheric palette, a vivid contemporary palette, or a refined gallery direction with a distinctive non-neutral accent system. These are illustrative categories, not preselected DAG directions.

## Reference study and originality
Study POJ Studio (https://pojstudio.com/) and Macaroni Shoten (https://shop.macaro-ni.jp/). Record concrete observations. Do not copy assets, exact layouts, copy or brand identity. Express DAG's own Jaisalmer identity.

## Required owner interaction
After showing all options:
- Ask the owner which territory to choose, combine or reject.
- Make options easy to compare.
- Do not hide trade-offs.
- Do not recommend, rank, score, badge, preselect or imply that one option is the default.
- STOP before finalizing tokens or production visual styling.

## After selection
1. Record exact selection and owner decision in `DECISIONS.md`.
2. Mark the system owner-approved.
3. Add approved tokens, font details and component guidance to `04-VISUAL-DESIGN-SYSTEM.md`.
4. Implement consistently.
5. Do not silently substitute colours or fonts.
6. Seek renewed approval only for foundational visual changes; routine component work can proceed within the approved system.

## Acceptance checklist
- [ ] Four or more genuinely distinct directions.
- [ ] Exact colour and typography specifications for every option.
- [ ] Visually rendered examples, not text alone.
- [ ] Desktop and mobile examples.
- [ ] No automatic neutral/grey direction.
- [ ] Trade-offs documented.
- [ ] Owner selection recorded.
- [ ] Production tokens match the selected direction.
