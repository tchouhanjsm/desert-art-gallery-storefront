# 11 — Customer and Merchandising Strategy

## Purpose
Define who DAG serves, what customers need to understand and how the catalogue should be presented. Do not invent personas or customer research.

## Market orientation
Japan-first is the intended customer focus. All customer-facing content remains English-only. Specific sales territories and legal/operational arrangements are recorded as unresolved where applicable.

## Customer research hypotheses
Research and validate:
- Interest in Indian/Jaisalmer textiles and objects.
- Purchase motivation: personal use, interiors, gifting, collecting or other.
- Expectations for material, dimensions, condition, provenance and care information.
- Trust questions when buying from an overseas gallery.
- Shipping, returns, payment and delivery concerns.
- Mobile discovery and comparison behaviour.
- Preferred support and enquiry expectations.

Label all personas and assumptions as hypotheses until supported by evidence.

## Merchandising principles
- Curate for a clear reason, not to fill a grid.
- Make category and collection differences understandable.
- Prioritize accurate, useful product details.
- Make one-of-a-kind, made-to-order, variant and unavailable states clear where they exist.
- Do not claim bestsellers, popularity or scarcity without data.
- Avoid empty sections and repetitive brand statements.
- Stories must add useful product context and never substitute for product discovery.

## Product taxonomy work
Propose a category structure based on the actual catalogue. Do not assume categories before inspecting products. Record taxonomy proposals, inclusion rules and owner approval.

## Commercial model to evaluate
Compare:
1. Direct ecommerce purchase.
2. Enquiry-led gallery.
3. Hybrid model where some items are purchasable and others require enquiry.

For each, assess customer experience, operational burden, data requirements, payment implications, inventory implications and trust. Do not implement until the owner selects a model.

## Merchandising acceptance
A prototype must show how a customer discovers categories, browses products, understands a product and knows the next action. Use real catalogue data when available; otherwise label sample content clearly as design-only.


## Catalogue fact preparation

Before defining final category names or building product pages, capture a private representative sample using the draft [catalogue intake standard](20-CATALOGUE-INTAKE-AND-FACT-VERIFICATION.md). Distinguish source-documented facts from owner-verified facts and public approvals. A candidate category is not an approved category. Use only verified product evidence when writing customer-facing copy.
