## 2. Eligibility and screening criteria

Proposed minimum criteria:
- Currently lives in Japan and can speak to their own shopping context in Japan.
- Aged 18 or older for this proposed adult-consent study; no upper age limit.
- Fits one of the four recruitment contexts above.
- Has researched, specified or purchased relevant goods recently (normally within 12 months for consumers; within 24 months for trade participants). The recruiter records only the qualifying context and date range, not a detailed purchase history.
- Can participate in a 30–45 minute remote or in-person discussion in a language supported by the authorized moderator.
- Has no direct commercial/recruitment relationship that would make their answers unduly influenced.

Do not screen on gender, ethnicity, religion, income, disability, political views or other irrelevant/sensitive traits. Do not collect exact birth date, full postal address, employer name, financial details or unnecessary contact data. Do not recruit minors. Screening answers should be limited to eligibility and quota.

## 3. Research objectives

1. Understand a recent real shopping trigger for textiles, interior objects, handmade goods or a considered gift.
2. Learn the words people naturally use for categories, materials, use contexts and styles.
3. Identify what photographs and image angles people need to judge form, scale, texture, condition and context.
4. Identify product facts they check (e.g. dimensions, material, care, condition, maker and origin) and which sources they trust.
5. Understand what makes a small or unfamiliar retailer feel credible, and what information is missing.
6. Understand how shoppers seek delivery, tax/duty, payment, cancellation/returns and support information when buying cross-border, without implying DAG has approved any such policy.
7. Learn when a shopper would contact a gallery and what useful information they would expect.
8. Identify contradictory evidence and differences between consumer and trade contexts.

The METI statistic is context only: Japan's 2024 domestic B2C e-commerce market was reported at ¥26.1 trillion, up 5.1% from 2023 (METI, 26 August 2025). It does not establish demand for DAG, Jaisalmer products or the accepted cohort. Source: https://www.meti.go.jp/english/press/2025/0826_003.html

## 4. Interview guide — 30 to 45 minutes

### Opening and consent (3–5 min)
- Thank the participant and explain the study purpose in plain language.
- Explain voluntary participation, note-taking, optional recording, confidentiality limits, retention and withdrawal route.
- Ask for explicit consent before any notes are linked to an identifiable contact record. Ask for separate, explicit permission before recording.
- Confirm that no purchase, sale or service is being offered as part of the research.

### Recent behaviour (8–10 min)
1. “Tell me about the last time you searched for or bought a textile, home object, handmade item or considered gift.”
2. “What started the search? What were you trying to find or solve?”
3. “Where did you look first, and what did you do next?”
4. “What did you compare or rule out? What made you stop looking?”

### Product discovery and evaluation (10–12 min)
5. “What words would you use to describe the kind of item you were looking for?”
6. “Which photographs helped you understand the item? What was missing?”
7. “What did you need to know about dimensions, material, care, condition or maker?”
8. “How did you decide whether the information was trustworthy?”
9. “What would make you uncertain about a product page or a small independent gallery?”

### Retail trust and cross-border context (6–8 min)
10. “Have you recently considered buying from a seller outside Japan? What did you check before deciding?”
11. “What information would you look for about the seller, delivery, taxes/duties, payment, returns and customer support?”
12. “Which unanswered question would stop you proceeding or make you contact the seller?”

### Contact and close (3–5 min)
13. “When do you contact a shop before making a considered purchase?”
14. “What information would make that conversation useful?”
15. “What have I not asked that mattered in your last purchase?”

Neutral probes: “What happened next?”, “Can you tell me more?”, “How did you check?”, “What did you do at that point?” Avoid “Would you buy this?”, leading references to POJ/Macaroni, forced ranking of imagined features and claims about DAG's current fulfilment or products.

## 6. Consent, privacy and data handling

Before fieldwork, prepare and owner-approve:
- A concise participant information sheet: who conducts the study, purpose, duration, voluntary nature, tasks, data collected, use, access, storage, retention/deletion, withdrawal and contact route.
- Separate affirmative consent for participation and for audio/video recording. Declining recording must not exclude an otherwise eligible participant; use notes only.
- A separate permission decision for any attributed or verbatim quotation. Default to paraphrase.
- A data-minimisation screener and anonymized participant IDs (R01–R07).
- A private, access-controlled storage location with named custodians and an agreed deletion date.
- A process for withdrawal requests and deletion of raw audio/video after synthesis. If full withdrawal becomes impossible after irreversible anonymisation, explain that limit before consent.
- A privacy/vendor review if a panel or external moderator processes personal information. Check current Japanese privacy requirements and the provider's security, subcontracting, transfer and retention terms before sharing data.

Do not put names, emails, telephone numbers, social handles, addresses, raw recordings, transcripts or identifiable notes into GitHub, this public repository, public issues/PRs or shared AI workspaces. Keep contact/booking information separate from research notes. Collect only information needed to answer the objectives. These are proposed safeguards, not legal advice or a determination of APPI compliance.

Reference guidance for the owner/legal review:
- Japan Personal Information Protection Commission, Laws and Policies: https://www.ppc.go.jp/en/legal/ (the Commission notes that the Japanese original legislation is authoritative).
- PPC, overview and handling of personal information: https://www.ppc.go.jp/en/ (review current applicable guidance before collection).
- ICC/ESOMAR International Code on Market, Opinion and Social Research and Data Analytics: https://iccwbo.org/news-publications/policies-reports/icc-esomar-international-code-on-market-opinion-and-social-research-and-data-analytics/ (voluntary informed participation, transparency and data responsibility).

## 8. Synthesis and reporting

Use anonymized IDs and report:
- Sample composition and recruitment route.
- Observed behaviour separately from reported past behaviour, inference and proposal.
- Evidence-linked findings, counter-evidence and cases that differ.
- Counts with denominator (e.g. “3 of 7 sessions”), not population percentages.
- Differences by context without creating personas from one interview.
- Implications for product facts, image needs, categories, trust information and useful contact.
- Limitations: seven purposively recruited Japan-based participants; not representative, not market sizing, not demand validation and not proof of conversion.
