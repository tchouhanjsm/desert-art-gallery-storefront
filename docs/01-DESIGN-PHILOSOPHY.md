# 01 — Design Philosophy and Working Mindset

## Purpose
Define how DAG thinks about product, design and service. This is a working philosophy, not a decorative theme guide.

## Japanese-inspired principles

### Omotenashi — thoughtful customer consideration
Anticipate customer questions and reduce effort. Make navigation, product details, availability, delivery information, checkout and support understandable. Avoid hidden surprises and unnecessary customer work.

### Shokunin — dedication to craftsmanship
Care about the finish of every detail: image crops, typography, spacing, product information, interaction feedback, error states, accessibility and operational reliability. "It works" is the beginning of acceptance, not the end.

### Ma — intentional space
Use whitespace to establish hierarchy, breathing room and focus. Space must serve content. An empty product grid, oversized blank hero or missing customer information is not Ma.

### Kanso — purposeful simplicity
Remove clutter, repetition and unnecessary controls. Never remove useful product information, shopping affordances or customer reassurance in the name of minimalism.

### Kaizen — continuous improvement
Observe actual use, inspect rendered pages, test assumptions, prioritize meaningful weaknesses, improve and verify. Do not make cosmetic changes merely to create activity.

## DAG-specific translation
Express these principles through DAG's own Jaisalmer material and craft identity. Do not use generic Japanese motifs, stereotyped cultural graphics, random Kanji, cherry blossoms, torii gates or red-sun imagery as shortcuts.

Authenticity requires verifiable source information.

## Creative standard
A page should feel considered, complete, useful and commercially coherent. Product discovery and presentation are central. Storytelling should deepen interest in products, not replace the store.

## Permanent principle
**DO NOT ABANDON MINIMALISM. ABANDON EMPTINESS.**

## Review questions
- Does every element have a clear purpose?
- Can customers understand what DAG offers?
- Do products receive enough visual attention?
- Is the page calm without being sparse?
- Is navigation intuitive?
- Are useful details easy to find?
- Is the design credible and distinctive to DAG?
- Does mobile feel intentionally composed?
- Are all claims supported by verified information?
