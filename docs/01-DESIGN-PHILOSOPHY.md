# 01 — Design Philosophy and Working Mindset

## Purpose
Define how DAG thinks about product, design and service. This is a working philosophy, not a decorative theme guide or authority to add UI beyond D-014.

## Japanese-inspired principles

### Omotenashi — thoughtful customer consideration
Anticipate customer questions and reduce effort. Make verified product information, currently supplied navigation and support information understandable. Future availability, delivery, checkout or service flows require separate commercial/legal approval; mentioning them here does not add them to the current kit.

### Shokunin — dedication to craftsmanship
Care about the finish of every detail: image crops, typography, spacing, verified product information, accessibility and operational reliability. "It works" is the beginning of acceptance, not the end.

### Ma — intentional space
Use whitespace to establish hierarchy, breathing room and focus. Space must serve content. An empty product grid, oversized blank hero or missing verified information is not Ma.

### Kanso — purposeful simplicity
Remove clutter, repetition and unnecessary controls. Never remove useful product information or customer reassurance in the name of minimalism. Do not add functionality beyond the closed v2 component inventory.

### Kaizen — continuous improvement
Observe actual use, inspect rendered pages, test assumptions, prioritize meaningful weaknesses, improve and verify. Do not make cosmetic changes merely to create activity.

## DAG-specific translation
Express these principles through DAG's own Jaisalmer material and craft identity. Do not use generic Japanese motifs, stereotyped cultural graphics, random Kanji, cherry blossoms, torii gates or red-sun imagery as shortcuts.

Authenticity requires verifiable source information. Customer-facing content remains English-only; Japan-first does not require Japanese-language UI.

## Creative standard
A page should feel considered, complete and useful. Product discovery and presentation are central. Storytelling should deepen interest in products, not replace the store. Follow D-014 and the current page/component inventories; do not add sections or functionality.

## Permanent principle
**DO NOT ABANDON MINIMALISM. ABANDON EMPTINESS.**

## Review questions
- Does every supplied element have a clear purpose?
- Can customers understand what DAG offers?
- Do products receive enough visual attention?
- Is the page calm without being sparse?
- Is supplied navigation intuitive?
- Are verified details easy to find?
- Is the experience credible and distinctive to DAG?
- Does mobile feel intentionally composed?
- Are all claims supported by verified information?
