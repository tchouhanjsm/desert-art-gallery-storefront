## 1. Owner direction

On 6 November 2026, the owner explicitly rejected all four visual territories in P-015: Graphic Atelier, Future Relics, Living Garden and Modern Folk Pop.

The owner first named these two sites as references (D-009). On 7 November 2026 the owner confirmed their relationship (D-010): BLEND, with POJ Studio primary and Macaroni Shoten secondary:

- https://pojstudio.com/
- https://shop.macaro-ni.jp/

The owner asked whether DAG can be designed "exactly as it is" in these references. The design scope is a close, original DAG adaptation of their retail quality, customer-experience patterns, information hierarchy and editorial discipline. It is not a pixel-for-pixel clone. Do not reproduce either site's logo, brand identity, copy, product photography, proprietary assets or exact page composition. DAG must use its own products, imagery, content and Jaisalmer identity.

This hierarchy settles which reference informs which part of the experience. It does not select a final visual style, palette, typography, image treatment, photography mood or production theme.

## 2. What the live references currently demonstrate

### POJ Studio — editorial artisan-commerce

Reviewed on 6 November 2026:
- Homepage: https://pojstudio.com/
- Tableware collection: https://pojstudio.com/collections/tableware-1
- Bestsellers: https://pojstudio.com/collections/bestsellers
- About and mission: https://pojstudio.com/pages/about

Observed patterns:
- A compact announcement area communicates a first-order promotion and a tariff/duty information link.
- Primary navigation separates Shop, Experiences, Kintsugi, Stories, Visit and About. Shop navigation groups categories, bestsellers and specialist collections.
- Country/currency and language controls are visible.
- The homepage combines a seasonal campaign hero, concise brand statement, category/collection discovery, mission content, press, artisan stories and bestseller merchandising.
- Collection pages use category groupings with contextual introductions, product grids or carousels, prices, variants and states such as Bestseller, Preorder and Sold out.
- The bestsellers collection exposes sorting, availability and price filtering, product counts and visible prices.
- About content develops the mission through artisan, team and sustainability stories.
- The footer provides category, company, FAQ and shipping/returns information.

DAG learning: editorial storytelling and commerce can coexist. Give shoppers several routes into the catalogue while connecting objects to makers, practices and context. Stories should deepen discovery, not obstruct it.

### Macaroni Shoten — compact curated-store structure

Reviewed on 6 November 2026:
- Live storefront: https://shop.macaro-ni.jp/
- Live Kitchen category: https://shop.macaro-ni.jp/categories/3902202
- 2021 launch announcement: https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000069.000005511.html (historical context only).

Observed patterns and limitations:
- The live storefront has a compact header/search and short Japanese introductory text.
- Its visible category taxonomy contains Kitchen, Living, Food and Daily, with category-specific sublinks.
- About, contact, privacy and commercial-disclosure paths are easy to locate.
- On the access date, the live homepage and Kitchen category both reported an empty-product state. Active product cards, filters, detailed product pages and checkout could not be evaluated.
- The 2021 launch announcement described a curated selection of Japanese handmade goods and selected food products. This is historical context, not proof of the current catalogue or operation.

DAG learning: category discovery can be compact and understandable. Define categories from DAG's actual catalogue and customer use; do not copy Macaroni's category labels or Japanese customer content.

## 3. Original DAG translation

These are requirements for the next design study, not an approved final layout.

### Home
- Make the actual DAG range and shopping path clear immediately.
- Use authentic DAG product photography after the owner selects a representative image sample.
- Provide direct category/collection entry points.
- Support curated edits without inventing products or availability.
- Connect relevant products to maker, material and craft stories.
- Include product discovery with truthful names, prices and stock states when the catalogue is ready.
- Keep service and contact information discoverable without publishing unapproved commitments.

### Shop and collection
- Keep taxonomy compact and understandable.
- Use short collection context where it helps shoppers.
- Show verified product imagery, names, prices, options and availability.
- Add filters and sorting only where the catalogue supports them.
- Never invent labels such as bestseller, handmade, limited or sustainable.

### Product detail
- Prioritize a clear image gallery and useful image sequence.
- Explain verified materials, dimensions, origin, maker, technique and care when the information exists.
- Connect products to relevant maker/craft context without copying POJ's content.
- Keep price, variants, availability and the purchase/enquiry action clear.
- Delivery, tax, duties, returns and payment statements remain gated by unresolved decisions U-001–U-006.

### Editorial and maker stories
- Give craft stories their own route.
- Link stories to relevant products/collections only where the relationship is verified.
- Use original DAG photography and owner-approved editorial content.

### Mobile
- Keep search, catalogue access and purchase/cart actions easy to find.
- Use a compact category hierarchy and usable filters.
- Preserve price and product facts; do not hide essential information behind decorative interactions.
- Review complete shopping journeys at the required viewports, not only the homepage hero.

## 4. DAG identity and content boundaries

- DAG remains rooted in Jaisalmer, Rajasthan, and its verified textiles, objects, materials and craft context.
- Japan-first remains the intended customer focus; customer-facing content remains English-only.
- Do not insert Japanese wording or motifs merely because the references are Japanese.
- Approximately 3,000 original photographs for roughly 300 products are recorded as available, but bulk migration remains deferred.
- Do not invent maker identities, provenance, techniques, product attributes, prices, inventory, sustainability claims or commercial policies.
- Do not commit the product-photo library or reference-site assets to Git.

## 5. Can DAG be designed exactly like the references?

A close adaptation of their broad retail principles is feasible: clear navigation, curated discovery, campaign-led first view, strong product imagery, editorial context, useful product information and a responsive shopping flow.

A direct clone is not the scope. Do not copy source code, logos, brand assets, writing, product photography, product content or pixel-for-pixel composition. DAG must have its own Jaisalmer identity and content.

## 6. Next design deliverable

Prepare a fresh owner-review package based on both references and DAG's actual product, brand and customer requirements. It must:
1. Present at least four materially different DAG-specific visual territories. Do not simply relabel or lightly edit rejected P-015.
2. Give each option exact HEX values and colour roles.
3. Specify display, heading, body and UI fonts, weights, availability and licensing.
4. Show home, shop/collection, product-detail and editorial composition.
5. Define photography direction using clearly labelled placeholders until an owner-approved product sample is supplied.
6. Include rendered desktop and mobile examples.
7. State neutral advantages, disadvantages, customer impression and accessibility/contrast implications.
8. Avoid recommendations, rankings, scores, preselection or an implied default.
9. Ask the owner to choose, combine, request revisions or reject all, then stop before final tokens or production styling.

P-015 is rejected and must not be reused as an active option set.
